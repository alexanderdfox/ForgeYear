# The Forgecycle Calendar
### Also called the Anvil Year

A calendar built around the full life of metal — from raw ore to finished blade.  
Time is measured in the same stages a smith uses: preparation, fire, force, and rest.

---

## Core Structure

| Unit              | Length          | Notes |
|-------------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Forgeyear**     | 360 days        | + 5–6 unnumbered Slag Days |
| **Heat** (month)  | 30 days         | 12 Heats per year |
| **Strike**        | 10 days         | 3 Strikes per Heat |
| **Week**          | 6 days          | Matches the classic smith’s rhythm |
| **Slag Days**     | 5 (or 6)        | Intercalary days outside the Heats |

**Deep Forge Year**: Every 4th year has 6 Slag Days instead of 5.

The Slag Days sit between the end of **Mark** and the beginning of **Ore**. They are formless, unnumbered days of rest, scrap, and renewal. No work is scheduled.

---

## The 12 Heats

| #  | Heat Name | Stage of Forging                          | Character |
|----|-----------|-------------------------------------------|---------|
| 1  | **Ore**   | Gathering and sorting raw material        | Dark, heavy, preparatory |
| 2  | **Bloom** | First smelting; spongy iron appears       | Birth of usable metal |
| 3  | **Spark** | Raising the fire, charging the forge      | Building heat and intent |
| 4  | **Scale** | Full forging temperature; oxide flakes fly| Raw power, surface transformation |
| 5  | **Strike**| Hammer on anvil; shape takes form         | The decisive heart of the year |
| 6  | **Draw**  | Lengthening and refining under controlled blows | Extension and refinement |
| 7  | **Quench**| Sudden cooling; hardness is locked in     | Sudden change, testing, finality |
| 8  | **Temper**| Careful reheating to reduce brittleness   | Resilience and balance |
| 9  | **Anneal**| Softening heat for further work           | Relief of stress, preparation for new work |
| 10 | **Grind** | Removing imperfections, revealing the edge| Precision and revelation |
| 11 | **Polish**| Final surface work; the metal begins to shine | Beauty and completion |
| 12 | **Mark**  | Stamping the maker’s mark, oiling, resting| Ownership, legacy, rest |

## The 6-Day Week

| Day     | Letter | Meaning                              |
|---------|--------|--------------------------------------|
| Anvil   | A      | Foundation. Planning, grounding, setting the work |
| Hammer  | H      | Force and decision. Making things happen |
| Tong    | T      | Handling and control. Precision with what is hot |
| Bellows | B      | Breath and fire. Energy, feeding the work |
| Quench  | Q      | Sudden change and testing. Results appear |
| Rest    | R      | The only true rest day. Cooling, oiling, quiet |

Days are commonly written with their letter:  
`A H T B Q R`

---

## Special Days & Observances

- **First Spark** (1 Spark)  
  The year is lit. Fires are kindled at dawn. People share the first heat of the season.

- **Full Strike** (mid-Strike Heat)  
  The longest continuous working stretch. Celebrated with communal forging or competitive hammering.

- **Quench Night** (last day of Quench)  
  Everything that has been worked is plunged into water or oil. A night of sudden decisions, endings, and cold clarity.

- **Temper Feast** (mid-Temper)  
  The balancing feast. Food is neither too hot nor too cold. People speak of resilience and second chances.

- **Marking Day** (last day of Mark)  
  The closing of the year. Maker’s marks are stamped, tools are cleaned and oiled, and the year’s work is formally set aside.

---

## How Time Feels

A person living by the Forgecycle does not count abstract numbers so much as they feel the metal’s temperature.

- Early Heats (**Ore → Scale**) are heavy and preparatory.  
- Mid-year (**Strike → Temper**) is the hot, loud, decisive core.  
- Late Heats (**Anneal → Mark**) grow quieter and more precise until the year ends in polished stillness.

Then come the formless Slag Days — the pause between one finished piece and the next raw ore.

The calendar teaches that nothing useful is made in a single heat.  
Every finished edge carries the memory of ore, fire, hammer, and rest.

*“The year is a single long heat. Live accordingly.”*
